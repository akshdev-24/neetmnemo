MMNEMONIC 11

"Oceans = 70% Area but ONLY 55 Billion Tons"
Oceans occupy ~70% of earth surface but contribute only

MMNEMONIC 12
Secondary Productivity Definition
"C-O-N: Consumers Organic New"
Rate of formation of new organic matter by Consumers.
MMNEMONIC 13
Photosynthetically Active Radiation (PAR)
"PAR < 50% Solar Radiation"
Less than 50% of incident solar radiation is PAR.
MMNEMONIC 14
PAR Captured by Plants
"Plants Capture 2 - 10% of PAR"
Plants capture only 2-10% of PAR (which equals 1-5% of

MMNEMONIC 15
Energy Flow Rule
"U-N-I: Unidirectional Non-cyclic Energy"
Energy flow in an ecosystem is strictly Unidirectional (Sun
→ Producers → Consumers).
MMNEMONIC 16
10 Percent Law
"Lindeman's 10% Transfer Limit"
Only 10% of energy is transferred to each trophic level from
the lower trophic level.
MMNEMONIC 17
Grazing Food Chain (GFC)
"P-H-C: Producer Herbivore Carnivore"

Detritus Food Chain (DFC)
"D-D-S: Dead Organic Detritivore Saprotroph"

PART 1: ORGANISMS & ABIOTIC FACTORS (MNEMONICS 1 – 10)
#01 	Levels of Biological Organization 	O - P - C - B
O – Organism (Basic functional biological unit)
P – Population (Group of individuals of same species)
C – Community (Interacting populations of different species)
B – Biome (Major regional ecological unit, e.g., Desert)
Ecology is studied at four main levels of biological organization.
#02 	Major Biomes of India 	T - D - R - S
T – Tropical Rain Forest
D – Deciduous Forest
R – Rain-shadow Desert (Arid regions)
S – Sea Coast / Coastal biomes
Temperature and annual precipitation variations create India's major biomes.
#03 	Key Abiotic Factors 	T - W - L - S
T – Temperature (Most ecologically relevant abiotic factor)
W – Water (Influences productivity and distribution)
L – Light (Drives photosynthesis and photoperiodism)
S – Soil (pH, grain size, and mineral composition)
These four key environmental variables dictate organismal survival and habitats.
#04 	Thermal Tolerance Ranges 	EURY (Wide) vs STENO (Narrow)
Eurythermal: Tolerates wide temperature range (e.g., Mammals, Birds)
Stenothermal: Restricted to narrow temperature range (e.g., Polar bears, Reptiles)
Prefix "Eury-" = Wide; Prefix "Steno-" = Narrow.
#05 	Salinity Tolerance in Aquatic Habitats 	Euryhaline vs Stenohaline
Euryhaline: Tolerates wide range of salinities (e.g., Salmon, Estuarine fish)
Stenohaline: Restricted to narrow salinity range (e.g., Goldfish, Oceanic sharks)
Inland water <5 ppt; Sea water 30–35 ppt; Hypersaline lagoons >100 ppt.
#06 	Responses to Abiotic Stress 	R - C - M - S
R – Regulate (Maintain internal homeostasis via physiological means)
C – Conform (Internal environment changes with surrounding environment)
M – Migrate (Move temporarily to hospitable conditions)
S – Suspend (Dormancy, Spores, Hibernation, Aestivation)
Four evolutionary options for organisms faced with stressful environments.
#07 	Why Conformers Dominate (Thermoregulation Cost) 	SMALL = HIGH S/V
Small size: High Surface Area to Volume ratio
Heat Loss: Lose body heat very fast in cold conditions
Metabolic Cost: Thermoregulation requires huge energy expenditure
Explains why small animals like hummingbirds and shrews are rare in polar regions.

PART 2: ADAPTATIONS & POPULATION ATTRIBUTES (MNEMONICS 11 – 20)
#11 	Xerophytic Plant Adaptations 	C - A - M - T - S
C – CAM photosynthetic pathway (Stomata open at night)
A – Absence of leaves / modified into spines (Opuntia)
M – Mucilage / fleshy stems for water storage
T – Thick cuticle on leaf surfaces
S – Sunken stomata to minimize transpiration loss
Structural and physiological xerophytic adaptations to arid desert conditions.
#12 	Allen's Rule for Cold Climate Mammals 	ALLEN = Short Extremities
A – Cold climate Mammals
L – Lower length of ears and limbs (Extremities)
L – Less heat loss minimized by lower Surface Area
E – Seals have thick fat layer (Blubber) as insulator
Morphological rule minimizing heat loss in cold climate mammals.
#13 	Biochemical Adaptations in Extreme Habitats 	A - F - B
A – Archaebacteria in hot springs/hydrothermal vents (>100°C)
F – Fishes in Antarctic waters (Antifreeze proteins prevent freezing)
B – Benthic marine invertebrates under high hydrostatic pressure
Biochemical variations allow survival in extreme physical conditions.
#14 	Population Attributes vs Individual Attributes 	B - D - S - A
B – Birth rate (Per capita births, not individual birth)
D – Death rate (Per capita deaths, not individual death)
S – Sex ratio (Percentage of males and females)
A – Age distribution (Pyramid representation of age groups)
Populations have collective attributes that individual organisms lack.
#15 	Age Pyramid Shapes & Growth Trends 	E - S - D
E – Expanding (Triangular pyramid: Pre-reproductive > Reproductive)
S – Stable (Bell-shaped: Pre-reproductive = Reproductive)
D – Declining (Urn-shaped: Pre-reproductive < Reproductive)
Age structure diagrams predict future population growth status.
#16 	Methods of Measuring Population Density 	P - C - T - N
P – Percent cover or Biomass (Used for single massive Banyan tree)
C – Count / Total Head Count (Direct census)
T – Trapping / Pug marks & fecal pellets (Tiger census)
N – Number per trap (Relative density for fish)
Absolute head count is often impossible; relative density methods are used.
#17 	Four Factors Regulating Population Density 	B - I - D - E
B – Births / Natality (+) [Increases density]
I – Immigration (+) [Increases density]
D – Deaths / Mortality (-) [Decreases density]
E – Emigration (-) [Decreases density]
Density formula: Nt+1 = Nt + [(B + I) - (D + E)].

PART 3: LIFE HISTORY & INTERSPECIFIC INTERACTIONS (MNEMONICS 21 – 30)
#21 	Reproductive Strategies (Breeding Frequency) 	ONCE vs MANY
Breed ONCE in lifetime: Pacific Salmon fish, Bamboo plant
Breed MANY times in lifetime: Most Birds, Mammals
Organisms evolve to maximize Darwinian fitness in their specific niche.
#22 	Offspring Number vs Size Strategy 	MANY-SMALL vs FEW-LARGE
Many small-sized offspring: Oysters, Pelagic fishes
Few large-sized offspring: Birds, Mammals (Elephants, Humans)
Trade-off between energy investment per offspring and total clutch size.
#23 	Interspecific Interactions Sign Matrix 	+ / - / 0
Mutualism (+/+): Both species benefit
Competition (-/-): Both species harmed
Predation & Parasitism (+/-): One benefits, one harmed
Commensalism (+/0): One benefits, one unaffected
Amensalism (-/0): One harmed, one unaffected
Standard ecological classification of two-species interactions.
#24 	Ecological Roles of Predators 	T - R - A - P
T – Transfer energy across trophic levels
R – Regulate prey population density (Prevents pest outbreaks)
A – Maintain species Diversity (Reduces interspecific prey competition)
P – Prudent nature (Avoids over-exploitation of prey)
Predators act as biological control agents and community stabilizers.
#25 	Pisaster Starfish Removal Experiment 	PISASTER = 10 Invertebrates Extinct
Location: Rocky intertidal communities of American Pacific Coast
Removal Effect: Over 10 species of invertebrates became extinct within a year
Mechanism: Extinction due to unchecked interspecific competition among prey
Classic ecological field experiment demonstrating predator-maintained diversity.
#26 	Invasive Species Example (Prickly Pear Cactus) 	CACTUS + MOTH
Invasion: Prickly Pear Cactus introduced into Australia (1920s) caused havoc
Biological Control: Controlled by introducing cactus-feeding Cactoblastis moth

#32 	Gause's Competitive Exclusion Principle 	G - A - U - S - E
G – Two closely related species competing for same limited resources
A – Cannot co-exist indefinitely
U – Competitive inferior will be eliminated eventually
S – Valid only when resources are limiting
Fundamental rule governing strict ecological niche overlap.

#31 Hallmarks of Cancer Cells 	L - O - S - S
• L: Loss of contact inhibition (normal cells stop dividing upon contact; cancer cells lose this)
• O: On-going uncontrolled cell division forming mass tumors
• S: Structural invasion of surrounding healthy tissues
• S: Unchecked proliferation forming neoplastic cells

#32 Benign vs Malignant Tumors & Metastasis 	B-C vs M-M
• Benign (B-C): Confined to original location, causes little damage
• Malignant (M-M): Mass of proliferating neoplastic cells
• Metastasis: Cancer cells slough off, travel via blood, and start new tumors in distant organs
Metastasis is the most feared property of malignant tumors.
#33 Carcinogens: Physical, Chemical & Biological 	P - C - B
• Physical: Ionizing (X-rays, gamma rays) & non-ionizing (UV) radiation
• Chemical: Tobacco smoke chemicals (major cause of lung cancer)
• Biological: Oncogenic viruses with viral oncogenes (v-onc) / cellular proto-oncogenes (c-onc)
Proto-oncogenes in normal cells get activated under carcinogen exposure to transform into active oncogenes.
#34 Cancer Detection & Diagnostic Techniques 	B - H - M - C - R

• M: MRI using non-ionizing radiation & strong magnetic fields
• C: Computed Tomography (CT) using X-rays for 3D imagery
• R: Radiography & Antibodies against cancer-specific antigens
MRI generates 3D images of internal soft tissues without harmful ionizing radiation.
#35 Cancer Treatment Modalities 	S - R - I - C
• S: Surgery: Physical removal of localized tumor
• R: Radiotherapy: Lethal irradiation of tumor cells saving normal tissue
• I: Immunotherapy: α-interferon biological response modifier activates immune system
• C: Chemotherapy: Anti-cancer drugs (side effects: hair loss, anemia)
α-interferon is given to cancer patients as it activates their immune system to destroy tumors.

#21 Key Concepts of Darwinian Theory 	BRANCHING DESCENT & NATURAL SELECTION

1. MENDELIAN GENETICS & 7 PEA PLANT TRAITS
#01 Mendel's 7 Pea Characters 	S - P - F - C - C - P - H
Stem height • Pod color • Flower color • Cotyledon color (seed) • Cotyledon shape (seed) • Pod shape • Hower position

#02 Dominant Traits in Pea Plant 	T - G - Y - R - I - F - A
Tall • Green pod • Yellow seed color • Round seed • Inflated pod • Flower axial • Alpha color (Violet flower)
These are the 7 dominant phenotypic traits expressed in the heterozygous F1 generation.
#03 Recessive Traits in Pea Plant 	D - Y - G - W - C - T - T
Dwarf stem • Yellow pod • Green seed color • Wrinkled seed • Constricted pod • Terminal flower • Terminal white flower

#04 Why Mendel Chose Pea Plants 	E - A - S - Y
Easily cultivated • Annual plant (short life cycle) • Self-pollinating (easily cross-pollinated) • Yields many offspring

#05 Mendel's Laws of Inheritance 	D - S - I
Dominance (Law of Dominance) • Segregation (Law of Segregation) • Independent Assortment
The foundational three principles established by Gregor Mendel using monohybrid and dihybrid crosses.
#06 Law of Segregation Rule 	P-U-R-E
Purity of gametes • Unblended alleles • Random fusion • Each gamete gets only one allele
Alleles do not show any blending; alleles segregate during gamete formation so each gamete carries only one factor.
#07 Monohybrid Phenotypic Ratio 	3 : 1
3 Dominant : 1 Recessive ratio observed in the F2 generation of a monohybrid cross.
Example: Tall (TT, Tt, Tt) to Dwarf (tt) plants in a 3:1 ratio.
#08 Monohybrid Genotypic Ratio 	1 : 2 : 1
1 Homozygous Dominant (TT) : 2 Heterozygous (Tt) : 1 Homozygous Recessive (tt)
Refers to the actual genetic constitution underlying the 3:1 phenotypic expression.
#09 Test Cross Purpose 	UNKNOWN GENOTYPE
Cross between an organism with dominant phenotype and a homozygous recessive parent to find unknown genotype.
If offspring show 1:1 ratio, the dominant parent was heterozygous; if 100% dominant, homozygous.
#10 Back Cross Definition 	F1 × ANY PARENT
Crossing of an F1 hybrid with either of its two parents (dominant or recessive).
All test crosses are back crosses, but not all back crosses are test crosses.

2. DIHYBRID CROSS & POST-MENDELIAN DEVIATIONS
#11 Dihybrid Phenotypic Ratio 	9 : 3 : 3 : 1
9 Both dominant • 3 Dominant-Recessive • 3 Recessive-Dominant • 1 Both recessive
Observed in F2 generation when tracking two independent pairs of contrasting traits (e.g., Round Yellow × Wrinkled Green).
#12 Dihybrid Genotypic Varieties 	TOTAL 9 GENOTYPES
Out of 16 boxes in Punnett square, there are 9 distinct genotypic combinations in standard dihybrid cross.
Ratio expression formula: (1:2:1) × (1:2:1) yields 9 different genotypes.
#13 Law of Independent Assortment Basis 	DIHYBRID CROSS
Based on dihybrid cross results where alleles of two different genes get sorted into gametes independently of one another.
Applies strictly to genes located on separate chromosomes or far apart on the same chromosome.
#14 Incomplete Dominance Example 	MIRABILIS JALAPA
Four O' Clock plant (Mirabilis jalapa) or Snapdragon (Antirrhinum majus) flower color.
Red (RR) × White (rr) yields Pink (Rr) flowers in F1, deviating from strict Mendelian dominance.
#15 Incomplete Dominance F2 Ratios 	1 : 2 : 1 (BOTH)
Both phenotypic and genotypic ratios in incomplete dominance are identical: 1 Red : 2 Pink : 1 White.
The heterozygous condition produces an intermediate blended phenotype.
#16 Co-Dominance Definition 	BOTH EXPRESSED EQUALLY
F1 generation resembles both parents equally without blending or dominance suppression.
Classic example: AB blood group in humans where both IA and IB alleles express simultaneously.
#17 Multiple Alleles Example 	ABO BLOOD GROUP
Controlled by gene I having three alleles: IA, IB, and i.

#18 Human ABO Blood Group Genotypes 	6 GENOTYPES / 4 PHENOTYPES
Genotypes: IAIA, IAi, IBIB, IBi, IAIB, ii (6 total yielding A, B, AB, O).
Demonstrates both multiple alleles (3 alleles) and co-dominance (IAIB).
#19 Pleiotropy Definition 	ONE GENE - MANY TRAITS
A single gene exhibits multiple phenotypic expressions (pleiotropic gene).

#20 Starch Grain Size in Pea (Pleiotropy) 	B GENE CONTROLS 2 TRAITS
Gene B controls starch grain size (large/small) AND seed shape (round/wrinkled) in pea plants.
Classic NCERT example showing how a single gene locus influences multiple phenotypic characters.

3. POLYGENIC INHERITANCE & CHROMOSOMAL THEORY
#21 Polygenic Inheritance Definition 	MANY GENES - ONE TRAIT
Traits controlled by three or more genes, producing a continuous range of expression (quantitative inheritance).
Example: Human skin color, height, and intelligence.
#22 Human Skin Color Inheritance 	A, B, C GENES
Controlled by 3 genes (A, B, C) with additive effect. Dominant alleles (ABC) = dark; recessive (abc) = light.
Produces a classic bell-shaped normal distribution curve in population phenotypes.
#23 Chromosomal Theory of Inheritance 	SUTTON & BOVERI (1902)
Proposed by Sutton and Boveri; paired condition of chromosomes is parallel to behavior of Mendelian factors.
Linked Mendelian hereditary units (genes) directly to physical chromosomes in the nucleus.
#24 Parallels Between Genes & Chromosomes 	C - O - U - P - L - E
Chromosomes & genes occur in pairs • Occur as alleles on homologous sites • Union of gametes restores pair • Pairs segregate
at meiosis • Linkage independent assortment • Equivalent behavior
The striking parallels confirmed chromosomes as the carriers of hereditary genetic information.
#25 Experimental Proof of Chromosomal Theory 	T.H. MORGAN (DROSOPHILA)
Demonstrated by Thomas Hunt Morgan using fruit fly (Drosophila melanogaster).
Drosophila was chosen due to short life cycle (2 weeks), cheap culture, and clear sexual dimorphism.
#26 Linkage Definition (Morgan) 	PHYSICAL ASSOCIATION
Tendency of genes located on the same chromosome to be inherited together during meiosis.
Violates independent assortment; linked genes show higher parental combinations than recombinants.
#27 Recombination Definition 	NON-PARENTAL GENE COMBINATIONS
Generation of non-parental gene combinations due to crossing over between non-sister chromatids.
Recombination frequency is directly proportional to the physical distance between genes.
#28 Tight vs Loose Linkage 	DISTANCE MATTERS
• Tightly linked genes: Low recombination, high parental progeny.
• Loosely linked genes: High recombination frequency.
Alfred Sturtevant used recombination frequency to construct genetic chromosome maps (centimorgans).
#29 Sex Determination Types 	XY - XO - ZW - H
XY (Humans/Drosophila) • XO (Grasshopper) • ZW (Birds) • Haplo-diploidy (Honeybee)
Different biological systems employ distinct chromosomal mechanisms to determine sex.
#30 Male Heterogamety Examples 	XX - XY & XX - XO
Males produce two types of gametes (X and Y, or X and O). Examples: Humans, Drosophila, Grasshoppers.
Female is homogametic (produces only X gametes).

Short Explanation: Apomictic seeds can form via adventitious embryony where nucellar cells surround and form embryos directly.

Short Explanation: Angiosperms exhibit a heteromorphic alternation of generations with a dominant diploid sporophyte and reduced
