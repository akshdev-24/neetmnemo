Chapter 21: Neural Control and Coordination (Mnemonics 1 – 6)
Topic - Human Neural System Subdivisions
Mnemonic: “CNS = Brain + Cord; PNS = Somatic (Skeletal) + Autonomic (Involuntary)”
- Central Neural System (CNS): Brain + Spinal Cord (site of information processing & control)
- Peripheral Neural System (PNS): All nerves connected to CNS (Afferent = sensory to CNS, Efferent = motor from CNS)
- PNS Subdivisions: Somatic Neural System (CNS to skeletal muscles) & Autonomic Neural System (CNS to involuntary organs/smooth muscles)
Structural and functional classification of the human nervous system.
Topic - Autonomic Nervous System Divisions
Mnemonic: “Sympathetic = Fight or Flight; Parasympathetic = Rest and Digest”
- Sympathetic Nervous System: Prepares body for stress/emergencies (dilates pupil, increases heart rate, inhibits digestion)
- Parasympathetic Nervous System: Promotes conservation of energy (constricts pupil, slows heart rate, stimulates digestion)
Antagonistic operational divisions of the visceral autonomic neural system.
Topic - Structure of a Neuron
Mnemonic: “Neuron = Soma (Cell Body with Nissl) + Dendrites (Inflow) + Axon (Outflow)”
- Cell Body (Soma): Contains cytoplasm, typical organelles, and Nissl's granules (granular protein-synthesizing bodies)
- Dendrites: Short, branching fibers containing Nissl's granules that conduct impulses towards cell body
- Axon: Long single fiber terminating in synaptic knobs, lacking Nissl's granules, transmitting impulses away from soma
Anatomical regions of a standard multipolar neuron and direction of impulse flow.
Topic - Types of Neurons by Structural Polarity
Mnemonic: “Unipolar = Embryo, Bipolar = Retina, Multipolar = Cerebral Cortex”
- Unipolar Neuron: Cell body with one axon only (found usually in embryonic stage)
- Bipolar Neuron: One axon and one dendrite (found in retina of eye, olfactory epithelium)
- Multipolar Neuron: One axon and two or more dendrites (found in cerebral cortex)
Morphological categorization of neurons based on number of processes emerging from soma.
Topic - Myelinated vs Non-Myelinated Nerve Fibers
Mnemonic: “Myelinated = Schwann + Myelin Sheath + Nodes of Ranvier (Cranial/Spinal Nerves)”
- Myelinated Fibers: Enveloped with Schwann cells forming myelin sheath; gaps between sheaths called Nodes of Ranvier (cranial and spinal nerves)
- Non-Myelinated Fibers: Enclosed by Schwann cell that does NOT form myelin sheath (autonomous and somatic neural systems)
Histological distinction and distribution of myelinated and unmyelinated axons.
Topic - Resting Membrane Potential (Polarization)
Mnemonic: “Resting State = K+ In, Na+ Out; 3 Na+ Pumped Out for 2 K+ In (-70 mV Inside)”
- Permeability: Axonal membrane at rest is more permeable to K+ and nearly impermeable to Na+ and negatively charged proteins
- Sodium-Potassium Pump: Actively transports 3 Na+ outward for every 2 K+ inward using ATP
- Result: Outer surface positively charged, inner surface negatively charged (Resting potential ~ -70 mV)
Electrochemical equilibrium establishing resting polarization in an unstimulated axon.

Chapter 21: Neural Control and Coordination (Mnemonics 7 – 12)
Topic - Action Potential and Depolarization
Mnemonic: “Stimulus -> Na+ Channels Open -> Rapid Na+ Influx -> Action Potential (+30 mV)”
- Stimulus Arrival: Membrane at site of stimulus becomes freely permeable to Na+
- Depolarization: Rapid influx of Na+ causes reversal of polarity (inner surface becomes positive, outer negative)
- Action Potential: The electrical potential difference across membrane at peak depolarization (~ +30 mV), constituting nerve impulse
Ionic event triggering generation of a self-propagating nerve impulse.
Topic - Repolarization Phase
Mnemonic: “Na+ Channels Close, K+ Channels Open -> K+ Efflux Restores -ve Potential Inside”
- Inactivation: Na+ channels close rapidly after fraction of a millisecond
- K+ Efflux: K+ channels open, permitting outward diffusion of K+
- Restoration: Restores resting negative potential on inside of membrane within milliseconds
Recovery phase returning the axonal membrane to its baseline resting electrical charge.
Topic - Saltatory Conduction
Mnemonic: “Saltatory = Impulse Jumps from Node to Node in Myelinated Axons”
- Process: Action potential leaps from one Node of Ranvier to the next in myelinated nerve fibers
- Advantage: Dramatically increases speed of impulse transmission (up to 50–100 times faster than unmyelinated fibers) and conserves ATP
High-speed node-to-node electrical propagation along insulated axons.
Topic - Synapse Types (Electrical vs Chemical)
Mnemonic: “Electrical = Direct Touch (Rare, Faster); Chemical = Synaptic Cleft + Neurotransmitters”
- Electrical Synapse: Membranes of pre- and post-synaptic neurons in close proximity (< 0.2 nm); current flows directly (rare in humans)
- Chemical Synapse: Separated by fluid-filled gap (synaptic cleft); requires chemical neurotransmitters (e.g., Acetylcholine)
Comparison of direct ionic current flow versus ligand-mediated synaptic transmission.
Topic - Mechanism of Chemical Synaptic Transmission
Mnemonic: “Impulse -> Ca2+ Influx -> Vesicles Fuse -> Neurotransmitter Released -> Post-Synaptic Receptor Binding”
- Step 1: Action potential reaches axon terminal (synaptic knob)
- Step 2: Voltage-gated Ca2+ channels open; Ca2+ influx causes synaptic vesicles to fuse with pre-synaptic membrane
- Step 3: Neurotransmitter released into cleft, binds to specific receptors on post-synaptic membrane, opening ion channels
Sequential molecular events converting electrical impulse to chemical signal and back.
Topic - Cranial Meninges (Outer to Inner)
Mnemonic: “DAP - Dura mater (Outer), Arachnoid (Middle), Pia mater (Inner)”
- Dura Mater: Tough, fibrous outermost layer in contact with skull
- Arachnoid: Thin, delicate middle layer with spider-web-like network
- Pia Mater: Extremely vascular innermost membrane in direct contact with brain tissue
Protective connective tissue envelopes surrounding central nervous system.

Chapter 21: Neural Control and Coordination (Mnemonics 13 – 18)
Topic - Brain Major Divisions
Mnemonic: “Forebrain (Cerebrum, Thalamus, Hypothalamus), Midbrain, Hindbrain (Pons, Cerebellum, Medulla)”
- Forebrain: Consists of Cerebrum, Thalamus, and Hypothalamus
- Midbrain: Located between thalamus/hypothalamus of forebrain and pons of hindbrain
- Hindbrain: Comprises Pons, Cerebellum, and Medulla Oblongata
Gross anatomical organization of human encephalon.
Topic - Forebrain: Cerebrum & Corpus Callosum
Mnemonic: “Corpus Callosum Connects Left & Right Cerebral Hemispheres”
- Structure: Deep longitudinal fissure divides cerebrum into right and left cerebral hemispheres
- Corpus Callosum: Thick tract of myelinated nerve fibers connecting the two cerebral hemispheres
- Cerebral Cortex: Outer layer of grey matter containing motor, sensory, and association areas
Interhemispheric commissural tract and functional organization of cerebral cortex.
Topic - Association Areas of Cerebral Cortex
Mnemonic: “Association Areas = Neither Purely Sensory nor Motor; Memory, Communication, Intersensory Associations”
- Definition: Complex non-specific regions of cerebral cortex
- Functions: Responsible for complex functions like intersensory associations, memory, and communication
Integrative cortical regions processing multi-modal sensory inputs and higher cognitive tasks.
Topic - Thalamus and Hypothalamus Functions
Mnemonic: “Thalamus = Sensory Relay; Hypothalamus = Thermoregulation, Hunger, Thirst, Hormones”
- Thalamus: Major structure wrapped by cerebrum; primary relay center for sensory and motor signaling
- Hypothalamus: Lies at base of thalamus; controls body temperature, urge for eating (hunger) and drinking (thirst), and secretes hypothalamic hormones
Diencephalic centers regulating sensory relay and homeostatic visceral controls.
Topic - Limbic System (Emotional Brain)
Mnemonic: “Limbic System = Amygdala + Hippocampus + Inner Cerebrum + Hypothalamus (Sex, Emotion, Motivation)”
- Components: Inner parts of cerebral hemispheres and associated deep structures (amygdala, hippocampus)
- Functions: Regulates sexual behavior, expression of emotional reactions (excitement, pleasure, rage, fear), and motivation
Subcortical circuit mediating emotional processing, memory consolidation, and drive behaviors.
Topic - Midbrain and Corpora Quadrigemina
Mnemonic: “Midbrain = Cerebral Aqueduct Passageway + Corpora Quadrigemina (4 Visual/Auditory Lobes)”
- Cerebral Aqueduct: Canal passing through midbrain carrying CSF
- Corpora Quadrigemina: Dorsal portion of midbrain consisting of four round swellings (lobes) controlling visual and auditory reflexes
Mesencephalic reflex centers and ventricular canal structure.

Chapter 21: Neural Control and Coordination (Mnemonics 19 – 24)
Topic - Hindbrain Components (Pons, Cerebellum, Medulla)
Mnemonic: “Pons = Fiber Tracts; Cerebellum = Balance/Coordination; Medulla = Respiration/Heart/Vomiting”
- Pons: Consists of fiber tracts interconnecting different regions of brain
- Cerebellum: Very convoluted surface to accommodate more neurons; maintains posture, body equilibrium, and precision movements
- Medulla Oblongata: Connected to spinal cord; contains vital centers controlling respiration, cardiovascular reflexes, and gastric secretions
Functional specializations of hindbrain and brain stem structures.
Topic - Brain Stem Structure
Mnemonic: “Brain Stem = Midbrain + Pons + Medulla Oblongata (Excludes Cerebellum)”
- Definition: Core structure connecting higher brain regions to spinal cord
- Constituent Parts: Midbrain, Pons, and Medulla Oblongata
- Function: Establishes connections between brain and spinal cord, housing fundamental autonomic reflex centers
Anatomical definition of brain stem excluding cerebellar structures.
Topic - Reflex Arc Pathway
Mnemonic: “Receptor -> Afferent Neuron -> CNS (Interneuron) -> Efferent Neuron -> Effector Organ”
- Receptor: Detects stimulus (e.g., knee jerk, hot object touch)
- Afferent (Sensory) Neuron: Transmits signal via dorsal root ganglion into spinal cord
- Efferent (Motor) Neuron: Transmits signal via ventral root from spinal cord to effector muscle/gland
Involuntary peripheral response pathway bypassing conscious brain processing.
Topic - Eye Wall Layers (Outer to Inner)
Mnemonic: “Outer Sclera/Cornea -> Middle Choroid/Ciliary -> Inner Retina”
- External Layer: Dense connective tissue called Sclera; anterior transparent portion called Cornea
- Middle Layer: Choroid (bluish, vascular layer); thickens anteriorly to form Ciliary Body and Iris
- Inner Layer: Retina containing three layers of neural cells (ganglion cells, bipolar cells, photoreceptor cells)
Concentric tunics forming the globe of human eye.
Topic - Retinal Cell Layers (Inner to Outer Direction of Light)
Mnemonic: “Inner Ganglion Cells -> Bipolar Cells -> Outer Photoreceptor Cells (Rods & Cones)”
- Layer 1 (Innermost): Ganglion cells whose axons form optic nerve
- Layer 2 (Middle): Bipolar neurons connecting photoreceptors to ganglion cells
- Layer 3 (Outermost): Photoreceptor cells (Rods and Cones) containing light-sensitive proteins (opsin and retinal)
Laminar neuronal arrangement of retina through which light passes before reaching photoreceptors.
Topic - Rods vs Cones (Photoreceptor Function)
Mnemonic: “Rods = Rhodopsin, Twilight/Scotopic (Night); Cones = Color, Daylight/Photopic (Day)”
- Rods: Contain purplish-red protein Rhodopsin (derivative of Vitamin A); responsible for twilight (scotopic) vision
- Cones: Responsible for daylight (photopic) vision and color vision (Red, Green, Blue cones)
- Fovea Centralis: Thinned-out area of retina packed exclusively with densely packed cones; point of highest visual acuity
Functional specialization of dim-light scotopic rods and high-resolution color cones.

Chapter 21: Neural Control and Coordination (Mnemonics 25 – 30)
Topic - Eye Chambers (Aqueous vs Vitreous)
Mnemonic: “Aqueous Humor = Anterior (Cornea to Lens, Fluid); Vitreous Humor = Posterior (Lens to Retina, Gel)”
- Aqueous Chamber: Space between cornea and lens filled with thin watery fluid (aqueous humor)
- Vitreous Chamber: Space between lens and retina filled with transparent gel (vitreous humor)
Anatomical fluid compartments maintaining intraocular pressure and shape of eye globe.
Topic - Mechanism of Vision
Mnemonic: “Light -> Retinal Dissociates from Opsin -> Membrane Permeability Changes -> Action Potential in Ganglion Cells”
- Light absorption induces dissociation of Retinal (aldehyde of Vit A) from Opsin protein
- Changes opsin structure, altering membrane permeability of photoreceptor cell
- Generates potential differences in bipolar cells, triggering action potential in ganglion cells propagated via Optic Nerve to Visual Cortex
Photochemical signal transduction steps converting light energy to visual nerve impulses.
Topic - Human Ear Structural Parts
Mnemonic: “Outer (Pinna/Meatus) -> Middle (Malleus, Incus, Stapes) -> Inner (Cochlea & Vestibular Apparatus)”
- Outer Ear: Pinna and external auditory meatus leading to tympanic membrane (eardrum)
- Middle Ear: Contains 3 ear ossicles - Malleus (Hammer), Incus (Anvil), Stapes (Stirrup - smallest bone)
- Eustachian Tube: Connects middle ear cavity with pharynx to equalize pressure on both sides of eardrum
Anatomical triad of human auditory system conducting sound vibrations.
Topic - Inner Ear Anatomy (Membranous and Bony Labyrinths)
Mnemonic: “Bony Labyrinth filled with Perilymph; Membranous Labyrinth filled with Endolymph”
- Bony Labyrinth: Series of bony channels surrounding membranous labyrinth, bathed in Perilymph
- Membranous Labyrinth: Located inside bony channels, filled with fluid called Endolymph
- Cochlea: Coiled portion of labyrinth containing organ of Corti
Fluid-filled chamber system housing auditory and equilibrium sensory hair cells.
Topic - Organ of Corti and Auditory Mechanism
Mnemonic: “Sound Waves -> Eardrum -> Ossicles -> Oval Window -> Perilymph -> Basilar Membrane Bends Hair Cells against Tectorial
Membrane”
- Location: Organ of Corti is located on Basilar Membrane inside Scala Media of Cochlea
- Hair Cells: Act as auditory receptors; stereo cilia pressed against overlying gelatinous Tectorial Membrane
- Impulse: Shear forces generate nerve impulses in afferent auditory nerve fibers to Auditory Cortex
Mechanoreception process converting fluid pressure waves into sound perception.
Topic - Vestibular Apparatus (Body Balance)
Mnemonic: “3 Semicircular Canals (Dynamic Balance - Crista Ampullaris) + Otolith Organs Sacculus/Utriculus (Static Balance - Macula)”
- Semicircular Canals: 3 perpendicular canals with swollen base (Ampulla) containing Crista ampullaris for dynamic balance
- Otolith Organ: Consists of Saccule and Utricle containing sensory ridge called Macula for static equilibrium and posture
Vestibular organ mechanics responsible for equilibrium and spatial orientation.

Mnemonic: "AMC Moves Fast"

2. MUSCLE STRUCTURE & SARCOMERE BANDS
Myofilament Banding: "Z-I-A-H-M"
Z-line: Anchors thin filaments.
I-band: Isotropic (Actin only, thin).
A-band: Anisotropic (Myosin overlap, thick).
H-zone: Center of A-band (Myosin only).
M-line: Middle membrane holding thick filaments.
Contraction Changes: "I Shorten, A Stays"
• I-band: Shortens during contraction.
• H-zone: Reduces / Disappears.
• A-band: Retains constant length.
• Z-lines: Pulled inward closer together.
3. MUSCLE FIBER TYPES COMPARISON
Feature 	Red Muscle Fibers (Aerobic) 	White Muscle Fibers (Anaerobic)
Myoglobin Content 	High (Red appearance) 	Low (Pale or whitish)
Mitochondria 	Abundant (High ATP output) 	Few in number
Sarcoplasmic Reticulum 	Less developed 	High / Highly developed
Respiration Mode 	Aerobic (Slow, sustained) 	Anaerobic (Fast, fatigues quickly)
4. CONTRACTILE PROTEINS STRUCTURE
Actin (Thin Filament): Composed of two double-helical F-actin polymer chains. Bound to regulatory proteins
Tropomyosin and Troponin (Troponin-T, Troponin-I, Troponin-C for Ca2+ binding).
Myosin (Thick Filament): Polymer of Meromyosins. Each meromyosin has a Heavy Meromyosin (HMM)
head/short arm with active ATPase activity and a Light Meromyosin (LMM) tail.

Chapter 19: Chemical Coordination and Integration (Mnemonics 1 – 6)
Topic - Definition and Modern Concept of Hormones
Mnemonic: “Hormones = Non-Nutrient Intercellular Messengers Produced in Trace Amounts”
- Traditional definition: Chemicals produced by endocrine glands poured directly into blood
- Modern definition: Non-nutrient chemicals acting as intercellular messengers produced in trace amounts
- Scope: Includes hormones secreted by organized endocrine glands as well as non-endocrine tissues (heart, kidney, GI tract)
Evolution of hormone definition incorporating local and systemic non-endocrine signaling molecules.
Topic - Major Endocrine Glands in Human Body
Mnemonic: “HPTP TAG O P Adrenal - Hypothalamus, Pituitary, Pineal, Thyroid, Thymus, Adrenal, Gonads, Pancreas”
- Hypothalamus, Pituitary, Pineal, Thyroid, Parathyroid, Thymus, Adrenal gland
- Pancreas (Islets of Langerhans) and Gonads (Testis in males, Ovary in females)
- Scattered endocrine cells also present in gastric, duodenal, renal, and cardiac tissues
Comprehensive anatomical checklist of primary ductless endocrine glands in humans.
Topic - Hypothalamus Regulatory Hormones
Mnemonic: “Releasing Hormones Stimulate, Inhibiting Hormones Stop (e.g., Somatostatin)”
- Releasing Hormones (RH): Stimulate secretion of pituitary hormones (e.g., GnRH stimulates gonadotrophins)
- Inhibiting Hormones (IH): Inhibit secretion of pituitary hormones (e.g., Somatostatin inhibits growth hormone)
- Portal System: Hypothalamic hormones reach anterior pituitary via hypophyseal portal system
Dual control mechanism exercised by hypothalamic neurosecretory nuclei over anterior pituitary.
Topic - Pituitary Gland Anatomy (Adenohypophysis vs Neurohypophysis)
Mnemonic: “Adenohypophysis = Pars Distalis + Pars Intermedia; Neurohypophysis = Pars Nervosa”
- Adenohypophysis (Anterior Pituitary): Divided into pars distalis and pars intermedia
- Neurohypophysis (Posterior Pituitary): Known as pars nervosa; stores and releases oxytocin and vasopressin
Embryological and structural division of the pituitary gland into anterior and posterior lobes.
Topic - Anterior Pituitary Hormones (Pars Distalis)
Mnemonic: “FLAT PEG - FSH, LH, ACTH, TSH, Prolactin, Endorphins/MSH, GH”
- TSH (Thyroid Stimulating Hormone), ACTH (Adrenocorticotropic Hormone)
- LH (Luteinizing Hormone), FSH (Follicle Stimulating Hormone)
- Prolactin (PRL) and Growth Hormone (GH)
- Pars Intermedia secretes Melanocyte Stimulating Hormone (MSH) in humans (merged with pars distalis)
Classic mnemonic for memorizing all six major anterior pituitary trophic hormones.
Topic - Growth Hormone (GH) Hypo & Hypersecretion Disorders
Mnemonic: “Dwarfism = Low GH Children, Gigantism = High GH Children, Acromegaly = High GH Adults”
- Dwarfism: Stunted growth due to hyposecretion of GH during childhood
- Gigantism: Abnormal overgrowth due to hypersecretion of GH during childhood
- Acromegaly: Disproportionate facial/limb enlargement due to GH hypersecretion in adults
Pathological consequences of growth hormone imbalance across different age stages.

Chapter 19: Chemical Coordination and Integration (Mnemonics 7 – 12)
Topic - Prolactin, TSH, and ACTH Functions
Mnemonic: “Prolactin = Milk Production, TSH = Thyroid Stimulator, ACTH = Adrenal Cortex Stimulator”
- Prolactin: Regulates growth of mammary glands and formation of milk in females
- TSH: Stimulates synthesis and secretion of thyroid hormones from thyroid gland
- ACTH: Stimulates synthesis and secretion of glucocorticoid hormones from adrenal cortex
Primary trophic functions of prolactin, thyrotrophin, and adrenocorticotrophin.
Topic - Gonadotrophins (LH and FSH) Functions
Mnemonic: “LH = Ovulation/Androgens, FSH = Spermatogenesis/Follicular Growth”
- Luteinizing Hormone (LH): Stimulates ovulation in females and synthesis/secretion of androgens (testosterone) in males
- Follicle Stimulating Hormone (FSH): Stimulates growth of ovarian follicles in females and spermatogenesis in males
Gonadotrophic regulation of gametogenesis and sex steroid synthesis in testes and ovaries.
Topic - Posterior Pituitary Hormones (Oxytocin and Vasopressin)
Mnemonic: “Oxytocin = Childbirth/Milk Ejection, ADH = Water Reabsorption in Kidney”
- Oxytocin: Contracts smooth muscles; stimulates uterine contraction during child birth and milk ejection from mammary glands
- Vasopressin (ADH): Acts mainly at renal tubules stimulating resorption of water and electrolytes, reducing water loss (diuresis)
Neurohypophyseal hormones synthesized by hypothalamus and released into posterior pituitary.
Topic - Diabetes Insipidus
Mnemonic: “DI = Low ADH -> Excessive Dilute Urine + Intense Thirst (Polyuria + Polydipsia)”
- Cause: Deficiency of Vasopressin / Antidiuretic Hormone (ADH)
- Symptoms: Inability of kidneys to conserve water leading to water loss, frequent urination (polyuria), and severe thirst
Endocrine disorder resulting from impaired water reabsorption in renal distal nephrons.
Topic - Pineal Gland and Melatonin
Mnemonic: “Pineal = Melatonin, Circadian Rhythm, Sleep-Wake Cycle, Pigmentation”
- Location: Dorsal side of forebrain
- Hormone: Melatonin (plays vital role in regulating 24-hour circadian rhythm of body)
- Functions: Maintains sleep-wake cycle, body temperature, metabolism, pigmentation, and defense capability
Epiphysis cerebri hormone dictating biological rhythms and photoperiodic responses.
Topic - Thyroid Gland Anatomy and Follicles
Mnemonic: “Thyroid = Isthmus Connects Lobes, Follicles Filled with Thyroglobulin + T3/T4”
- Structure: Two lobes on either side of trachea connected by non-secretory tissue called isthmus
- Histology: Composed of follicles and stromal tissues; thyroid follicles filled with colloid (T3 and T4)
Gross and microscopic structure of the butterfly-shaped endocrine gland in the neck.

Chapter 19: Chemical Coordination and Integration (Mnemonics 13 – 18)
Topic - Thyroid Hormones (T3, T4) and Iodine Requirement
Mnemonic: “Thyroxine Requires Iodine; Regulates Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR), RBC Formation”
- Requirement: Iodine is essential for normal rate of hormone synthesis in thyroid
- Functions: Regulates BMR, supports RBC formation, controls carbohydrate, protein, and fat metabolism
- Thyrocalcitonin (TCT): Protein hormone secreted by thyroid reducing blood calcium levels (hypocalcemic)
Metabolic control and iodine dependence of thyroid follicular hormone synthesis.
Topic - Hypothyroidism and Goitre / Cretinism
Mnemonic: “Hypothyroidism = Goitre (Low Iodine), Cretinism (Stunted Child), Myxedema (Adult)”
- Simple Goitre: Enlargement of thyroid gland due to dietary iodine deficiency
- Cretinism: Hypothyroidism during pregnancy causing stunted growth, mental retardation, abnormal skin
- Myxedema: Hypothyroidism in adult women causing low BMR and weight gain
Clinical syndromes resulting from inadequate thyroid hormone production.
Topic - Hyperthyroidism and Exophthalmic Goitre (Graves' Disease)
Mnemonic: “Graves = Bulging Eyes (Exophthalmos) + High BMR + Weight Loss”
- Cause: Hypersecretion of thyroid hormones due to thyroid cancer or nodules
- Symptoms: Enlarged thyroid, high BMR, increased heart rate, nervousness, weight loss, protruding eyeballs
Autoimmune hyperthyroid condition characterized by accelerated metabolism and exophthalmos.
Topic - Parathyroid Glands and PTH (Collip's Hormone)
Mnemonic: “PTH = Increases Blood Ca2+ (Hypercalcemic), Stimulates Bone Resorption”
- Location: 4 small glands stuck on back side of thyroid lobes
- Parathyroid Hormone (PTH): Peptide hormone increasing blood calcium levels (hypercalcemic factor)
- Action: Stimulates bone resorption (demineralization), promotes Ca2+ reabsorption by renal tubules and absorption from digested food
Calcium homeostasis regulator acting antagonistically to thyrocalcitonin.
Topic - Thymus Gland and Thymosins
Mnemonic: “Thymus = Behind Sternum, Thymosins Mediate Cell-Mediated Immunity (T-Lymphocytes)”
- Location: Lobular structure located between lungs behind sternum on aorta
- Hormone: Thymosins (peptide hormones playing major role in cell-mediated immunity)
- Differentiation: Promotes differentiation of T-lymphocytes for cell-mediated immunity and antibodies for humoral immunity
Immuno-endocrine gland promoting T-cell maturation that degenerates with age.
Topic - Adrenal Gland Anatomy (Cortex and Medulla)
Mnemonic: “Adrenal on Kidneys: Outer Cortex (3 zones), Inner Medulla (Adrenaline)”
- Structure: Situated on anterior top of each kidney; composed of outer cortex and inner medulla
- Cortex Zones (Outer to Inner): Zona glomerulosa (mineralocorticoids), Zona fasciculata (glucocorticoids), Zona reticularis (sex corticoids)
Zonal organization and dual embryological origin of adrenal glands.

Chapter 19: Chemical Coordination and Integration (Mnemonics 19 – 24)
Topic - Adrenal Cortex Hormones (Glucocorticoids, Mineralocorticoids)
Mnemonic: “Cortisol = Gluconeogenesis, Aldosterone = Na+ Retention & Water Balance”
- Glucocorticoids (Cortisol): Involved in carbohydrate metabolism, stimulates gluconeogenesis, lipolysis, proteolysis, anti-inflammatory
- Mineralocorticoids (Aldosterone): Regulates water and electrolyte balance; acts at renal tubules to reabsorb Na+ and water, excrete K+/PO43-
- Androgenic Corticoids: Secreted in small amounts, stimulate axial/pubic hair growth during puberty
Primary steroid hormones secreted by adrenal cortex regulating metabolism and electrolytes.
Topic - Addison's Disease and Cushing's Syndrome
Mnemonic: “Addison = Hypo-cortisol (Low BP, Pigmentation), Cushing = Hyper-cortisol (Moon face, High BP)”
- Addison's Disease: Underproduction of hormones by adrenal cortex causing hypoglycemia, weakness, skin pigmentation
- Cushing's Syndrome: Overproduction of cortisol causing obesity, moon face, high blood pressure, hyperglycemia
Clinical contrasting disorders of adrenal cortical insufficiency versus excess.
Topic - Adrenal Medulla Hormones (Catecholamines)
Mnemonic: “Adrenaline + Noradrenaline = Fight or Flight Hormones (Emergency Response)”
- Hormones: Adrenaline (Epinephrine) and Noradrenaline (Norepinephrine) collectively called catecholamines
- Trigger: Secreted in response to stress, emergency, fear, or danger (Fight or Flight)

Mnemonic: “Resting = High Na+ Outside, High K+ & Negative Proteins Inside (-70 mV)”
- Axoplasm: High concentration of K+ and negatively charged proteins, low concentration of Na+
- Extracellular Fluid: High concentration of Na+, low concentration of K+
- Permeability: Axolemma is more permeable to K+, nearly impermeable to Na+ and negatively charged proteins
- Result: Outer surface positive, inner surface negative (Polarised resting potential ~ -70 mV)
Ionic distribution across resting axonal membrane producing baseline electric polarization.
Topic - Sodium-Potassium Pump (Na+/K+ ATPase)
Mnemonic: “3 Na+ Out, 2 K+ In (3 NO 2 KIN)”
- Transport: Active transport against concentration gradient requiring ATP
- Stoichiometry: Pumps 3 Na+ outwards for every 2 K+ pumped inwards into the cell
- Role: Maintains ionic gradients across resting membrane

Mnemonic: “Stimulus -> Na+ Channels Open -> Rapid Na+ Influx -> Depolarisation (+30 mV)”
- Trigger: Threshold stimulus applied at site A makes membrane freely permeable to Na+
- Influx: Rapid influx of Na+ causes reversal of polarity (inner surface becomes positive, outer negative)
- Action Potential: Electrical potential difference across membrane during depolarisation (~ +30 mV / Nerve Impulse)
Mechanism of threshold voltage-gated sodium channel activation reversing membrane polarity.
Topic - Repolarisation Phase
Mnemonic: “Na+ Channels Close -> K+ Channels Open -> K+ Efflux Restores Resting Potential”
- Inactivation: Rise in Na+ permeability is extremely short-lived; Na+ channels close rapidly
- Efflux: Voltage-gated K+ channels open, K+ diffuses OUTSIDE axolemma
- Result: Restores negative potential inside membrane back to resting state
Efflux of potassium ions re-establishing resting electronegativity inside the axon.
Topic - Conduction of Impulse Along Axon
Mnemonic: “Current Flows Inside A to B, Outside B to A (Circuit Complete)”
- Inner Surface: Current flows from depolarised site A to adjacent site B
- Outer Surface: Current flows from site B to site A to complete local circuit
Local current circuits propelling action potential sequentially along the nerve fiber length.

Topic - Inspiration Mechanism
Mnemonic: “Active, Diaphragm Contracts Down, EICM Ribs Up, Volume Up, Pressure Down”

- Pressure Gradient: Intra-pulmonary pressure exceeds atmospheric pressure, expelling air
Passive elastic recoil mechanism expelling air from the lungs.
Topic - Forced Expiration Muscles

- Abdominal Muscles: Contract to push diaphragm upward, further decreasing thoracic volume
Active muscle involvement during forceful, non-resting exhalation.

Mnemonic: “M-E-M = Meristematic, Elongation, Maturation”
- Meristematic Phase: Dense protoplasm, large nuclei, abundant plasmodesmata
- Elongation Phase: Increased vacuolation, cell enlargement, new cell wall deposition
- Maturation Phase: Maximal cell wall thickening and protoplasmic modifications
Three sequential anatomical zones/phases of cellular growth behind a growing apex.
Topic - Arithmetic Growth Equation & Curve
Mnemonic: “Lt = L0 + rt (Linear Curve)”
- Lt: Length at time 't' | L0: Length at time 'zero' | r: Growth rate/elongation per unit time
- Division Pattern: Following mitotic division, only ONE daughter cell continues to divide while the other differentiates (e.g., root elongation)
Mathematical representation of constant growth rate resulting in a straight linear graph.
Topic - Geometric Growth Equation & Sigmoid Curve
Mnemonic: “W1 = W0 e^rt (Sigmoid S-Curve)”

- Phases: Lag phase → Log/Exponential phase → Stationary phase
- Graph: Classic Sigmoid (S-shaped) curve characteristic of all living organisms in natural environments
Exponential cell multiplication where both daughter cells retain capacity to divide.

- Redifferentiation: Dedifferentiated meristem loses division capacity to mature again (e.g., Secondary xylem/phloem, Cork)

Morphological flexibility in leaf structure adaptation across phases or habitats.
